Voice Activity Detection (VAD) & Analog Switching Pipeline

Explore how human speech (AC sound waves) is amplified, converted into a proportional DC voltage signal, and evaluated by Arduino to drive power speakers.

01

Electret Mic Input

Acoustic voice pressure creates small AC audio signal (~20mV–50mV).

Input: 20Hz–8kHz Audio
02

LM358 Preamp

Dual Op-Amp amplifies weak mic AC signal by ~100x gain up to ~2.5V AC.

Gain: 40dB (LM358P)
03

Peak Detector (DC Conv.)

1N4148 Diode + RC filter rectifies AC voice into a **smooth DC Voltage** (0.2V–4.5V DC).

Output: DC Envelope Signal
04

Arduino ADC & Logic

Reads DC levels on A0–A5, resolves room priorities, sets CD4051 select pins (S0,S1,S2).

Cycle Time: 10ms Fast Loop
05

LM386 Output & Speaker

CD4051 routes analog audio to target room's LM386 power module & 8Ω speaker.

Power: 2W Mylar Cone

Real-Time Signal Oscilloscope & DC Level Comparator

Channel A: Audio AC Waveform (Op-Amp Output)
Channel B: Converted DC Envelope Signal (VAD Peak Output)

Step-by-Step Manual DIY Fabrication & Wiring Guide

Complete instructions to build the physical mini-house prototype model and solder the electronic hardware.

1

Phase 1: Scale Mini-House Architecture (4BHK + Hall + Kitchen)

Construct the physical 3D structural model using 5mm Sunboard / Foam Core Board or Cardboard:

  • Cut baseboard measuring 60cm x 40cm to represent the floorplan.
  • Divide into 6 distinct rooms: Master Bedroom (20x15cm), Living Hall (25x20cm), Kitchen (15x15cm), and 3 standard bedrooms.
  • Drill 12mm circular cutouts in each ceiling/wall section for mounting 8Ω Mylar Speakers and 6mm Electret Microphones.
  • Run 2-core shielded twisted pair wiring along hidden floor channels back to the central circuit hub box.
2

Phase 2: Building the Electret Preamp & Peak Detector (AC to DC VAD)

Assemble the Voice Activity Detection circuit per room station on a Zero PCB:

[Electret Mic] ---> [LM358 Pin 2/3 Op-Amp Preamp (Gain ~100)] ---> [1N4148 Diode Anode] | +---> [10uF Capacitor + 100k Resistor to GND] ---> [Arduino Analog Pin A0-A5]
  • Electret mic outputs ~20mV AC sound waves when spoken into.
  • LM358 amplifies AC voice signal up to 2.5V peak.
  • 1N4148 diode rectifies positive half cycles, charging the 10uF capacitor to create a smooth DC Voltage Level (Proportional to speech volume).
3

Phase 3: Wiring the CD4051BE Analog Multiplexer Matrix

Connect the CD4051BE IC to control analog audio routing digitally via Arduino:

  • Connect CD4051 Pin 16 (VDD) to +5V and Pin 8 (VSS/VEE) to GND.
  • Connect Select Pins S0 (Pin 11), S1 (Pin 10), S2 (Pin 9) to Arduino Digital Pins D2, D3, D4.
  • Connect Common In/Out Pin 3 to LM386 Audio Input.
  • Connect Channels Y0–Y5 (Pins 13, 14, 15, 12, 1, 5) to individual room microphone outputs.
4

Phase 4: Power Amplification & Speaker Distribution

Setup LM386 modules to drive destination speakers clearly:

  • Power LM386 modules from 12V DC power adapter for maximum acoustic volume.
  • Connect LM386 output terminals directly to 8 Ohm / 2W speakers in each room.
  • Use 10k trimmers on the LM386 inputs to calibrate volume levels and prevent audio distortion/feedback.
5

Phase 5: Flashing Arduino Code & Calibration

Upload the priority state machine firmware to Arduino Uno/Nano:

  • Calibrate ADC noise floor threshold (typically 400–500 out of 1023 analog read).
  • Test Priority Matrix: Verify that speaking in Master Bedroom (A0) forces CD4051 to switch channels immediately, overriding Kitchen or Bedroom calls.
